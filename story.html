Where it began

A pretty pair in Gorkha Bazaar

Sushma Shrestha grew up in Tanahun, raised by her mother. After finishing +2, she was visiting Gorkha Bazaar when she found a pair of slippers so well made she couldn't stop looking at them. She bought them, and decided she wanted to make her own.

Her maternal aunt in Gorkha taught her the basics in a single day. Back in Pokhara she talked it over with her elder sister, bought materials, and practised at home. Her first try: two pairs.

Friends and relatives loved them and asked for their own. Money was tight and materials were expensive, so Sushma made one or two pairs at a time, sold them, and put every rupee back into the next batch. Sometimes her mother helped out. After six months, she had saved enough to buy materials in bulk.

One video changed everything

Sushma started posting her slippers on Instagram, then TikTok. One video brought in an order for 12 pairs from a single place, which she made and delivered in four days. After two years of small orders she went fully commercial and registered the business as Orange Handicraft Industry (अरेन्ज हस्तकला उद्योग).

Why “Orange”? It's her favourite colour, and every pair is handmade. Simple as that.

A day at the bench

There's no factory. The workshop is a room at home where Sushma cuts soles and uppers one day, then glues, stitches and packs the next, about 20 pairs every two days. Her days start around 6 in the morning and often end after midnight in festival season.

Today Orange makes around 300 pairs a month. They're sold through Instagram, Facebook and TikTok, stocked by shops in Pokhara, and delivered to customers in Butwal, Biratnagar, Bharatpur, Kathmandu and beyond. Nepalis abroad have carried them to Japan, Korea, Dubai, Australia and Croatia. Many customers come back for a second pair, because the slippers are affordable and they last.

“No work is big or small. But every work must be done honestly.”“काम सानो-ठूलो हुँदैन। तर हरेक काम इमानदार भएर गर्नुपर्छ।”Sushma Shrestha

Passing it on

Local women started coming to Sushma to learn the craft, so she began teaching. Orange now runs hands-on slipper-making training, and every graduate leaves with a certificate. The next dream: more helpers at the bench, more designs, and a showroom of our own.
